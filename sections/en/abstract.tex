\begin{abstract}
Speculative decoding (SD) accelerates generation by verifying several draft tokens in
one target pass. In a memory-constrained mixture-of-experts (MoE) model, however,
verification exposes a wider union of routed experts precisely when only a small
fraction of the BF16 expert pool fits in GPU memory. Independent draft weights,
duplicate sequence state, and full-BF16 miss traffic can therefore consume the
benefit of fewer target invocations. We present \system, a coupled execution system
that uses target-derived W4 experts both to draft tokens and as the base for exact
target-expert reconstruction. Compact corrections recover requested BF16 experts
bitwise, while unified W4--BF16 residency and one physical KV trajectory reuse state
across proposal, rollback, verification, and commit. We compare against Classic
MoE-SubSpec, SS-MoE, and SpecMoE on four MoE families and four workloads, and test
the design on three GPUs and 8--24~GiB memory budgets. At 8~GiB, \system delivers a
1.96$\times$ four-family geometric mean over Classic MoE-SubSpec, reaching
2.57$\times$/2.12$\times$/1.64$\times$/1.65$\times$ on
Mixtral/DeepSeek/Qwen/OLMoE; cross-platform results remain above the matched Classic
baseline, while exact recovery reduces transferred expert bytes by 38\%.
\end{abstract}
